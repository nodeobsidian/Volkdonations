Where It Began

A Problem Too Big
to Ignore

In 2009, Volk Donations co-founder Daniel Volk spent three weeks volunteering at an underfunded orphanage in Nairobi, Kenya. What he encountered — children sleeping four to a bed, classrooms without textbooks, medical needs going unmet for months — was not a crisis born of scarcity. It was a crisis born of neglect.

He returned to the United States and spent the following year in conversation with international development experts, social workers, and former NGO directors. The consensus was consistent: the infrastructure existed to change outcomes for orphaned children. What was lacking was reliable, transparent, targeted funding from people who genuinely cared.

Volk Donations was incorporated in Delaware in March 2010. The founding charter committed the organisation to three non-negotiable principles: transparency in all financial reporting, local partnership in all programme delivery, and zero tolerance for administrative waste above 4%.

"We did not start a charity. We built a bridge — between people with the means to give, and children with the right to thrive."
— Daniel Volk, Co-Founder, Volk Donations (2010)

Purpose & Direction

What We Stand For

🔭

Our Vision

A world in which no child's future is determined by the circumstances of their birth. We envision a global community where orphaned and vulnerable children have equal access to education, healthcare, nutrition, and the dignity of a safe home — not as charity, but as an unquestioned right.

🎯

Our Mission

To mobilise transparent, accountable funding from individuals, families, and organisations worldwide, and to channel that funding — through rigorously selected local partners — into programmes that deliver measurable, lasting improvement in the lives of orphaned children across the developing world.

💡

Our Purpose Statement

We exist because the systems that should protect the most vulnerable children have failed them. Volk Donations does not seek to replace those systems. We seek to demonstrate — one child, one community, one programme at a time — what is possible when generosity is paired with rigour. Every decision we make, from which programmes to fund to how we report our finances, is made in the service of that purpose.

Principles

Our Core Values

Six principles that are not aspirational statements but operational commitments, embedded in how we hire, fund, report, and operate every single day.

01
🔍

Radical Transparency

We publish full audited financials annually, programme-level expenditure quarterly, and impact metrics in real time through our donor portal. We hold a GuideStar Platinum rating and maintain administrative costs below 4%. Donors have the right to know exactly where every dollar goes — and we make that effortless.

02
🤝

Local Partnership First

We do not impose solutions from the outside. Every programme Volk funds is designed, staffed, and led by organisations based in the communities they serve. Our role is to provide resources, accountability frameworks, and strategic support — never to override local knowledge with external assumptions.

03
📊

Evidence-Driven Decisions

Programme funding is not awarded on sentiment or narratives alone. Every initiative Volk supports must demonstrate measurable baseline conditions, a credible theory of change, and a reporting framework that allows us to evaluate impact against stated objectives. We fund what works, and we stop funding what does not.

04
🛡️

Child Safeguarding Above All

Every partner organisation we work with must meet our Child Safeguarding Standards, independently developed in consultation with UNICEF guidelines and child protection specialists. No programme receives continued funding without annual safeguarding review. The protection of the children we serve is the non-negotiable floor beneath every other consideration.

05
🌱

Long-Term Impact over Short-Term Optics

We do not run short-term relief operations for the purpose of fundraising photography. Our programmes are designed with 3–5 year minimum horizons, because sustainable change in child welfare requires sustained investment. We measure success in outcomes — school enrolment rates, health indicators, long-term child safety — not in donation volumes.

06
⚖️

Dignity in Everything

The children and families we work with are not recipients of charity. They are rights-holders. Every communication we produce — public or internal — is reviewed to ensure it portrays programme beneficiaries with full dignity, avoiding the exploitation of vulnerability that is sadly common in fundraising. We raise money through truth, not pity.
